Haspr uses end-to-end encryption to protect your credentials. This means your passwords are encrypted on your
device before they leave it, and only you (and the people you choose to share with) can decrypt them. Not even the
server administrator or Haspr's cloud hosting team can read your data.

:::tip
For the full technical details, see the [Haspr security page](https://doc.haspr.dev/security).
:::

## What data is encrypted?

Haspr encrypts two categories of data:

- **Secrets**: passwords, TOTP codes, secret notes, and custom field secret values are always encrypted, regardless of
  configuration. Only the people a resource is shared with can decrypt them.
- **Metadata**: resource names, URLs, descriptions, custom field searchable keys, and icons can also be encrypted when
  using the v5 resource format. Whether
  metadata encryption is enabled depends on your organisation's configuration. See the
  [metadata encryption admin guide](/admin/resource-types/encrypted-metadata/) for more information.

## Where does encryption happen?

All encryption and decryption happens locally in your browser extension or mobile app, never on the server. The
server only ever receives and stores ciphertext (encrypted data it cannot read).

The browser extension is cryptographically signed, so your browser can verify it has not been tampered with before
running it. To learn more about why a browser extension is required, see
[Why do I need a browser extension?](/user/faq/why-a-browser-extension/).

## How is data encrypted?

Haspr uses the OpenPGP standard, a widely used open encryption standard. Here is how it works
at a high level:

- Every user has a **key pair**: a public key (shared with others) and a private key (kept secret).
- When someone shares a password with you, Haspr encrypts a copy of that secret with **your** public key. Only
  your private key can decrypt it.
- Each user who has access to a shared resource receives their own individually encrypted copy.
- Secrets are also digitally signed, so you can verify they came from a trusted sender and have not been altered.

## What is your private key?

Your private key is generated during your initial account setup. It is stored locally on your device and is never
sent to the server in cleartext.

Because your private key is what allows you to decrypt your passwords, losing it means losing access to your data.
There are two ways to recover if that happens:

- **Recovery kit**: during setup you can download a file containing your private key. Store it somewhere safe and
  use it to set up your account on another device. See
  [Account recovery setup](/user/settings/browser/account-recovery-setup/) for instructions.
- **Account recovery with admin approval**: if your organisation has enabled this feature, an administrator can
  help you regain access. Your private key is backed up (encrypted) on the server during enrolment, and an admin
  must approve the recovery request before you can set a new passphrase. See
  [Account recovery admin guide](/admin/account-recovery/) for instructions.

## What is your passphrase?

Your passphrase is what unlocks your private key on your device. It is never sent to the server. Without your
passphrase, nobody can use your private key to decrypt your passwords, even if they have access to your device.

Because your passphrase is known only to you, it cannot be reset by an administrator in the traditional way. If you
forget your passphrase, you must go through the account recovery process described above.

## Additional security features

Beyond end-to-end encryption, Haspr includes several layers of defense:

- **Anti-phishing token**: Haspr uses an anti-phishing token (a visual icon and colour combination) shown during
  login. If the token doesn't match what you expect, you may be on a spoofed site.
- **Role-based access control (RBAC)**: administrators can define roles and permissions to restrict who can create,
  share, or delete credentials. See [Roles and rights](/admin/users-rights/roles/) for details.
- **Audit logs**: administrators can review detailed logs of security-relevant events, such as logins, permission
  changes, and password access. See [Audit logs](/admin/audit-logs/) for details.
- **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)**: organisations can require TOTP, Duo, or YubiKey authentication for an
  extra layer of security at login. See [MFA setup](/user/settings/browser/mfa/) for details.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can server administrators read my passwords?

No. Passwords and secrets are encrypted on your client device before they are sent to the server. The server stores
only the encrypted ciphertext. Even a database administrator with direct access to the database cannot read your
passwords without access to your private key and passphrase.

### What happens if I lose my device?

Your passwords are safe because your private key on that device is protected by your passphrase. To regain access
on a new device, use your recovery kit or request account recovery through your administrator.

### Can Haspr (the team) access my data?

No. If you use Haspr Cloud, your data is stored in an encrypted database that Haspr engineers cannot decrypt. If you
self-host Haspr, your data never leaves your infrastructure.

### Is metadata encrypted too?

In v5 and later, metadata encryption can be enabled by an administrator to encrypt resource names, URLs, descriptions,
and custom fields. In earlier versions or when metadata encryption is disabled, metadata is sent in cleartext to
allow for server-side searching. See [Encrypted metadata](/admin/resource-types/encrypted-metadata/) for details.
